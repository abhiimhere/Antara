The installation एक स्वर, दो रूप

Same melody. Two instruments.

Antara was presented at Carnegie Library, Dún Laoghaire, in a hall with high ceilings and wooden floors. At the centre stood a freestanding, double-sided structure carrying two paintings and two speakers, one facing each half of the room.

The paintings showed the goddess Saraswati in two forms. One is painted in fluid, curved brushwork that mirrors the continuous motion of microtonal ornamentation. The other is a cubist fragmentation of the same image. Beneath them, a peacock blue silk saree was draped in a wave. A maroon carpet with symmetric patterns, electric candles and lamps gave the room the meditative stillness the music needed.

A Z Game Audio Visualizer running in FL Studio shifted the room's light with each raga, a visual cue that never named what was playing. A screen showed the interview footage, and an iPad running GeoShred beside a real flute let visitors feel the difference with their own hands.

Listen सुनिए

Flip between the two sides, mid-phrase.

Both recordings run together in perfect sync. Switch sides at any moment and hear the same melody lose, or regain, its motion, with the room's ambience underneath, as visitors heard it. Headphones help.

The three ragas तीन राग

Three emotional worlds.

Three melodic frameworks from Indian classical music, each a different argument about what microtonal identity means.

I

Darbari Kanada

दरबारी कानडा

Heavy, late-night and deeply serious. Its notes are almost those of a Western major scale, yet it sounds nothing like one. Its identity rests on the slow andolan of the flattened third and sixth. Remove those oscillations and the raga disappears.

II

Jog

राग जोग

Pentatonic and deeply touching, using both a flattened and a natural third. Specific rules govern how notes are approached going up and coming down, and those rules give Jog its feel.

III

Kafi

राग काफी

A third emotional world, a contrast to the gravity of Darbari and the intimacy of Jog. Its microtonal character drew the starkest difference between the two sides in audience responses.

About परिचय

Abhinav K Jayan

Sound designer, composer and Atmos mixer, studying on the Erasmus Mundus Joint Masters in Sound (ReSound) across IADT Dublin, Universidade Lusófona Lisbon and LMTA Vilnius. Trained in sound recording and design at the Film and Television Institute of India.

Antara grew from a long fascination with Indian classical music and the spaces between notes, the microtonal world no Western notation fully captures. It was a first attempt to make that world audible to anyone willing to walk through it.
